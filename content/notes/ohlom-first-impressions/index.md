Every few years someone promises "one app for everything". Most of the time it ends up being one app for nothing in particular. So I opened [Ohlom](https://ohlom.com/en/) with healthy scepticism — and closed it impressed. My first touch was good, and it met every expectation I had.

## What Ohlom is

*Ohlom* (ໂອ້ລົມ) is the Lao word for talking together, and the app is built around that idea: a conversation is where everything starts. Around chat it adds the other things people in Laos use every day:

- **Chat and calls** — one-to-one and group messages, voice and video calls, all end-to-end encrypted.
- **Community** — a feed with posts, photos, polls and 24-hour stories; follow people, creators and shops.
- **Video and live** — short vertical videos, live streaming, and gifts to support creators.
- **Shop** — a marketplace with live shopping, business storefronts, delivery or pickup, and order tracking.
- **Everyday services** — restaurants and table booking, events and QR tickets, jobs with a built-in CV builder, property listings, and appointment bookings.
- **For business** — a free business page, product catalogue, orders, bookings, staff attendance, timesheets and leave, and an offline POS with barcode and thermal receipts, manageable from a computer at biz.ohlom.com.

## What impressed me

**It is genuinely made for Laos.** Lao is the default language (English is there too), prices are in kip, and addresses support the full province–district–village structure. That sounds small; it is exactly the kind of detail imported apps never get right.

**Privacy is the default, not an option.** Messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted using the Signal protocol, and the service is designed so that Ohlom itself cannot read your chats. There are hidden chats behind a secret code, an app lock with PIN or fingerprint, safety-number verification, and — very welcome here — warnings before you tap known scam links.

**It looks and feels polished.** The design is clean and modern, and it runs on phone and tablet. A local product that does not look "local" in the bad sense is a statement in itself.

**It takes small businesses seriously.** A shop can open a page, sell, take bookings, go live, run its POS and track staff attendance in one place. For many small businesses in Laos, that replaces a pile of separate tools — or a notebook.

**It is free.** Download it and use it; open a business page and start selling.

## Congratulations to MTS

Building a product like this in Laos takes courage, not just code. Congratulations to the team at [MTS — Multimedia & Technology Solutions](https://mts.la/) on Ohlom. After nearly ten years of building websites and apps for others, this is a product that puts Lao software on the same screen as the global giants — and holds its own.

## A friendly word from one geek to another

Dear MTS team: I counted. The features page promises **"100+ features for your life and your business"**. In one app I found the job of a messenger, a social network, a short-video platform, a live-shopping channel, an online marketplace, a restaurant and table-booking app, an event-ticketing system, a jobs board with a CV builder, a property portal, an appointment scheduler, an HR attendance and leave system, and a point-of-sale.

It is impressive. It is also a gentle reminder: **you cannot host every feature of every app in the world inside one app** — and you don't need to. Every feature added is a feature to secure, maintain, test on old phones and explain to new users. The strongest super apps win by doing their core extremely well — and Ohlom's core, *the conversation*, is already excellent.

So please: polish the core, make it fast on the cheapest phones in the market, and finish the payment features (the site says they are "coming soon"). And leave a little room on our phones for the other apps — at least for now.

## Verdict

Ohlom is a confident, well-designed, privacy-first app built for how people in Laos actually live and do business. My first impression: it met — and in places exceeded — every expectation I had. I'll be watching how it grows, and I hope Lao users and businesses give it a serious try.

*Ohlom is free on iOS and Android — see [ohlom.com](https://ohlom.com/en/).*
